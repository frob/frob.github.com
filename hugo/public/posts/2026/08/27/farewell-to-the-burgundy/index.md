Last week I [wrote a list]({{< ref "2026-08-18-moving-on.md" >}}) of everything I need to update. Second item: personal site. Here is what has been sitting at [www.frankrobertanderson.com](http://www.frankrobertanderson.com) this whole time.

![The frankrobertanderson.com homepage: a dark burgundy background, olive header bar, and a typewriter-font nameplate](/images/2026/08/27/frankrobertanderson.png "Professional Drupal/Web Development, since 2009.")

Look at that footer. *© Copyright 2009.*

That site is old enough to drive. It was built on a version of Drupal that has been end-of-life for years, on a theme I wrote myself, in an era when a dark burgundy background and a typewriter-font nameplate read as *tasteful*. There is a "Socialize" tab in the nav. It links to services that in some cases no longer exist.

And the tagline: "To Provide Complete Drupal Development Solutions." I remember writing that sentence. I remember being pleased with it. It is the voice of someone who thinks a personal site is a storefront and that the storefront needs signage.

I have kept it alive out of something between loyalty and inattention. Every couple of years I would log in, apply a security update, look at the burgundy, think *I should really redo this*, and close the tab. It became a thing I maintained rather than a thing I used. The blog moved here years ago. The actual work moved to GitHub and GitLab. What remained was a nameplate over an empty shop.

That drawing in the middle -- the figure hunched over a desk, seen from above -- I still like. That one is coming with me.

The rest is going. Not because it is embarrassing, exactly. It is a genuine artifact of what web development looked like when I got serious about it, and I am not the sort of person who pretends the earlier versions did not happen. But a site that says 2009 in the footer is not telling anyone what I can do in 2026. It is telling them I stopped.

So it gets replaced. Something static, something I can rebuild in an afternoon, something that says what I actually work on now instead of what I hoped to sell seventeen years ago.

Seventeen years is a good run for a homepage. Longer than most jobs.
